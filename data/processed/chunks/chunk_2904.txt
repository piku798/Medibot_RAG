. Glucosamine is not readily available from any pri- mary food source. Commercial preparations of glu- cosamine are derived from chitin, which is a substance found in the outer covering of such shellfish as lobster, crab, and shrimp, as well as in such animal connective tissues as the marrow of chicken bones. Commercially prepared glucosamine comes in three formats: glu- cosamine sulfate, glucosamine hydrochloride, and N- acetyl-glucosamine (NAG)
