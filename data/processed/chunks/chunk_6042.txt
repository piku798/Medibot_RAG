In 2003, a group of Japanese researchers noted that the blood serum of patients with JRA contains elevated levels of interleukin-6, a cytokine (nonantibody protein) that is critical to regulation of the immune system and blood cell formation. Because interleukin-6 is also asso- ciated with inflammation, the researchers think that com- pounds inhibiting the formation of interleukin-6 might provide new treatment options for JRA
