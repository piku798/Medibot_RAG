. Esophageal manometry uses a thin flexible catheter placed down the esophagus. Small openings in the catheter sense pressure at various points on the esopha- gus while the muscle is at rest and during swallowing. The pressures are transmitted to a computer that ana- lyzes the wave patterns. An upper gastrointestinal (GI) series, or “barium swallow,” can reveal esophageal narrowing, ulcerations, tumors, hiatal hernia, or reflux episodes as they occur
