. Some thera- pists also use guided imagery in group settings. Guided imagery is a two-part process. The first com- ponent involves reaching a state of deep relaxation through breathing and muscle relaxation techniques. Dur- ing the relaxation phase, the person closes her eyes and focuses on the slow, in and out sensation of breathing. Or, she might focus on releasing the feelings of tension from her muscles, starting with the toes and working up to the top of the head
