. During the Depression, the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA) im- ported kudzu for erosion control. In 1972, the USDA classi- fied kudzu as a weed because the plant can reach 60 ft (18.29 m) in a single growing season. In June and July, the vines sport purple flowers and in autumn, the leaves shed. The kudzu root, which can grow to the size of a human being, has a history of use in Chinese medicine
