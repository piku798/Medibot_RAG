. In Hodgkin’s lymphoma, certain characteristic cells—Reed-Sternberg cells—must be present in order to confirm the diagnosis. These cells usually contain two or more nuclei—oval centrally-located structures within cells that house their genetic material. In addition to the identification of these Reed-Sternberg cells, other cells in the affected tissue sample are examined. The charac- teristics of these other cells help to classify the specific subtype of Hodgkin’s lymphoma
